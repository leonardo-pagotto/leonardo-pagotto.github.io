## Who I am

A good part of my adolescence went into programming. I grew up as the son of a retired developer, in what I like to call a programming family, so following that path felt less like a choice and more like a quiet duty.

If you asked me to describe my first contact with programming, the honest word is *healthy*, or *okay*. I know that is a disappointing answer. I was expecting something more dramatic from myself too.

My father bought an Arduino when I was twelve. Two years later I came home from school and he asked me to sit with him and watch a beginner Python video. I have been learning programming ever since. That is the part of the story worth keeping.

At eighteen I got my first job as a junior .NET full-stack developer at a financial institution, skipping the usual entry-level step. I learned a lot there, and I made friends for life. Two years later I wanted to see how other teams and other companies actually work, so I moved. A year and a half after that, I left.

## How I like things to be

I do not like things that are more complicated than they need to be. When something is too complicated, it usually means someone made it that way.

I will try to write in that same spirit: plainly, about what I am learning, and about the person I am becoming while I learn it.
